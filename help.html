How to use

Everything you need to get this on screen, and what to check when it misbehaves.

1. Put it on your stream

In OBS: Sources → + → Browser. One per row below.
Leave “Shutdown source when not visible” unchecked. With it on, OBS destroys the page whenever the scene is inactive and rebuilds it later, which is the one setting that can make a working overlay look broken.

The sizes above are starting points. The Customize tab measures what your card actually needs and tells you the exact number to use — a browser source larger than its content just leaves dead space you have to position around.

2. The tabs

Choose source — which player the overlay follows. Leave it on Automatic unless something keeps stealing the overlay; then pin the one you want.

Layouts — the four song styles side by side when you just want to compare and grab a URL.

Customize — every look setting, with a live preview, for all three sources. Change things, then Copy URL and paste that into OBS — or press Save look to keep it.

Saved looks — every look you saved, ready to copy back into OBS.

Themes — recolour the whole app and every OBS source in one click, or build and share your own.

Chat bot — commands the bot answers in your chat.

3. Twitch alerts and follower boxes

Optional — the song overlay works without any of this.

Open the setup page — it walks you through creating the Twitch app, has a Connect with Twitch button that handles sign-in for you, and ends with test buttons and the OBS URLs. No tokens to paste, and the connection renews itself.

Sign in as the channel owner when Twitch asks — follower events are read as the broadcaster, so any other account will not work. The Customize tab shows the live connection state and counts events as they arrive, so you can confirm it works without waiting for a real follower.

4. The chat bot

Also optional, and off until you switch it on.

Open the Chat bot tab and press Set up the bot — it signs in through Twitch the same way the alerts did. When Twitch asks which account, sign in as the account the bot should speak as: a separate bot account, or your own if you want replies to come from you. Nothing to paste, and the sign-in renews itself.

Then flip the switch. Five commands are on by default (!song, !uptime, !followage, !commands, and !so for mods). The rest ship switched off because they still contain placeholder text — fill them in, then turn them on.

Use Try a command to test anything without posting to chat. If the token is wrong, the tab names the exact missing scope rather than just failing.

Something looks wrong

Nothing on screen at all? Check Choose source first. A source pinned in “only” mode hides every other player, and that setting is saved per computer — so a machine can be pinned even when yours is not.

Overlay looks empty in OBS but fine here? That is usually the shutdown setting above, or a browser source sized much larger than the card.

Moved it to another computer? Check the version matches. It is in the tray menu and at the top of the diagnostics page; an older copy will be missing whole pages.

The diagnostics page answers most of this in one screen — what it can see, what it chose, and why iTunes did or did not connect. It ends with a block of text you can copy and send to someone.
